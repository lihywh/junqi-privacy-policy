How do I start a nearby match?

Use two Android devices. Connect both to the same Wi-Fi or hotspot, or choose Bluetooth. One player creates a room and the other finds and joins it. Allow the relevant system permissions, compare the same six-digit code, then both confirm and prepare a formation. For Wi-Fi, the host can show a room QR code and the guest can select Scan to join. Both must already use the same network; the code does not share a Wi-Fi password. Bluetooth rooms show the host device name. After a radio restart, check both phones for a system pairing notification and compare its codes; contact sharing is unnecessary.

The room does not appear.

Make sure the host has created a room. Guest or public Wi-Fi may isolate devices; try a personal hotspot or Bluetooth. Wi-Fi also supports a manual host IP and port. On Bluetooth, complete Android pairing on both devices; granting nearby permission does not complete pairing by itself.

What happens when the connection drops?

The app pauses the nearby match and attempts to recover the same session for up to 60 seconds. If recovery fails or a process is closed, the match is interrupted rather than counted as a win or loss. Bluetooth recovery still depends on the devices and system behavior.

Why do enemy pieces stay hidden in recaps?

Junqi is a hidden-rank game. A recap uses public events and the player’s known board, and does not disclose enemy identities that were never public.

How do I change the language or play on a small screen?

Open Settings to choose the system language or a supported app language. Board zoom and scrolling keep all stations reachable on short screens; tablets use a large board and compact side panel.
